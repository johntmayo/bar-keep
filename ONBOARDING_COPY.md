\# Onboarding Copy — The Architecture of the Glass  
\#\#\# Three screens. First launch.

\*\*\*

\#\# Design Notes for Implementation

These three screens are designed for first launch only. They should be skippable, and the final screen should have a clear CTA into the app. Each screen is intended to occupy most of the viewport with a single focused idea — no more than one concept per screen. The manifesto ("Go Deeper") should be accessible from within the app's About or Learn section, not from onboarding.

Recommended interaction pattern: swipe or tap to advance, dot pagination, skip link in top-right corner.

\*\*\*

\#\# Screen 1 of 3 — The Problem With Names

\*\*Headline:\*\*  
Most cocktail lists are just lists.

\*\*Body:\*\*  
You learn the Negroni. Then the Boulevardier. Then the Old Pal. Three separate things to memorize — when really, they're one idea expressed three ways.

This app organizes drinks by how they're built, not what they're called. Once you see the structure, you don't just know more drinks. You understand them.

\*\*Visual direction:\*\*  
Three drink cards fanned out — Negroni, Boulevardier, Old Pal — with a subtle connecting element or label indicating they share the same structural family.

\*\*CTA:\*\* Next →

\#\# Screen 2 of 3 — Structure First

\*\*Headline:\*\*  
Every cocktail is a balancing act.

\*\*Body:\*\*  
At the heart of every well-made drink is a structural logic — the relationship between its ingredients. Spirit and sweetener. Spirit and citrus. Spirit, fortified wine, and a bitter that earns its place.

We organize drinks into ten families based on that logic. Not by spirit. Not by era. By how the drink actually works.

\*\*The ten families:\*\*

\- Old-Fashioned  
\- Aromatized  
\- Bitter Aromatized  
\- Sour  
\- Daisy  
\- Enhanced Sour  
\- Collins  
\- Highball  
\- Rich & Creamy  
\- Tropical / Punch-derived

\*\*Visual direction:\*\*  
A clean typographic list of the ten families — no icons, no illustration. The list itself should feel like a taxonomy, not a menu. Considered spacing, restrained.

\*\*CTA:\*\* Next →

\#\# Screen 3 of 3 — Three Layers

\*\*Headline:\*\*  
Family. Template. Experience.

\*\*Body:\*\*  
Every drink in this app has three things:

A \*\*family\*\* — the structural logic underneath.  
A \*\*template\*\* — the classic it descends from.  
\*\*Tags\*\* — how it feels to drink.

Take the Negroni. It belongs to the Bitter Aromatized family. Its template is the Negroni. Its tags include spirit-forward, bitter, and aperitivo. That tells you everything: how it's built, where it comes from, and what it's like.

Now you can explore by structure when you want to learn, by experience when you just want a drink, and by template when you want to know what's in the neighborhood.

\*\*Visual direction:\*\*  
A single drink card — the Negroni — with three labeled rows visible beneath it: Family / Bitter Aromatized, Template / Negroni, Tags / Spirit-forward · Bitter · Aperitivo. Clean, minimal. The card should feel like a preview of the actual drink page UI.

\*\*CTA (primary):\*\* Start Exploring  
\*\*CTA (secondary, small):\*\* Read the full theory →  \*(links to manifesto)\*

\#\# Tone Notes

These screens should feel like the opening of something intelligent, not a tutorial or a feature list. The voice is confident and slightly spare — it assumes the user is curious and capable of handling a new idea. No exclamation points. No "Welcome to \[App Name\]\!" No feature bullets.

The goal is not to explain the app. It is to make the user feel that they are about to see cocktails differently.
